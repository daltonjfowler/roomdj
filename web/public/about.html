RoomDJ

Class song voting, minus the bad words. Students suggest songs and vote on what plays next from their Chromebooks. The teacher's computer plays the winners. A filter keeps it school-clean.

🔊 One speaker, not thirty. The music plays out loud from the teacher's computer only. Nothing streams to students' Chromebooks or headphones: their screens are just for picking and voting.

🍎 Start a room (teachers) 🎒 Join a class (students)

Why I made it

I'm Dalton, a high school engineering and CS teacher in New Jersey. Music in the room makes work time better. Taking requests out loud does not: half the class shouts, the other half asks for songs I cannot play at school, and I end up DJing instead of teaching. So I built a thing. Kids pick, kids vote, the filter says no so I don't have to.

How a class works

  1. The teacher opens a room at roomdj.com/host and signs in with Spotify. The class code goes up on the board.
  2. Students join at roomdj.com with the code. They get a made-up nickname like "Brave Otter". No accounts, nothing to type about themselves.
  3. They suggest and vote. Every suggestion is checked first. The song with the most votes plays next on the teacher's computer.
  4. At the end, a quick recap: the class's Top 5, who picked the crowd favorite, a few fun stats.

📺 Projector on a different computer? Open the board at roomdj.com/board on the projector computer. It shows the class code, what's playing and the votes, and plays nothing itself, so the music can come from your laptop or any other device.

What keeps it school-clean

  • Explicit versions are swapped for the clean version, or blocked when there isn't one.
  • Word lists, plus your own blocked words and banned songs.
  • An AI check reads what each new song is about (drugs, drinking, violence and so on), and checks album covers too. When it isn't sure, the song waits for the teacher in "Needs a look" instead of slipping through.
  • The teacher can veto, ban, lock suggestions, or play a pick of their own any time.

The checker is good, not perfect. It is not a replacement for a teacher in the room.

How a song gets checked

  1. The quick stuff first. The explicit flag, the word lists, your blocked words and your bans. No AI needed.
  2. The AI takes a guess from the title, artist and album. A guess isn't good enough to play, though.
  3. Then it reads the real lyrics. RoomDJ looks the song up on LRCLIB (a free, public lyrics library), the AI reads them once, and they get thrown away. RoomDJ never downloads, saves or hosts lyrics. The only thing kept is the answer: fine, not for school (and why, in a few kid-safe words), or not sure.
  4. Still not sure, or no lyrics out there? It waits in "Needs a look" and you decide. When in doubt, it waits for you instead of playing.

One honest exception: Kidz Bop. Their whole thing is rewriting songs for kids, so when the AI says a Kidz Bop song is fine, it doesn't wait on a lyrics lookup too. If the AI says no, it's still a no. (Teachers who feel strongly about Kidz Bop, either way, can set them to always allowed or always blocked in Settings.)

Kids can see where their song is at. In search, every song has a tag: ✓ Checked (plays right away), Not checked yet (it gets checked when they suggest it) or Needs teacher OK. That way nobody thinks "Clean edit" means "good to go". If their song is waiting on a check, they see why, their spot in line, and how long it's been. Only their own songs, never anyone else's.

The song archive

Every answer the checker gives goes into one shared archive, so a song only has to be checked once. Lyrics don't change. The more classes use RoomDJ, the more songs show up already ✓ Checked, and the fewer kids wait. Basically crowdfunding appropriateness.

  • What's in it: the song (title, artist, album, length) and the answer. No lyrics, no students, no nicknames, no rooms.
  • The night shift. The lyrics library gets busy during the school day, so some songs can't be double-checked in class. From 10 pm to 6 am Eastern, RoomDJ quietly checks them: songs that got stuck in class, and songs kids searched that weren't checked yet. (The top charts get pre-checked all day, before kids even search for them.)
  • Banned songs. When a teacher removes or bans a song the checker never finished, the night shift checks it properly, and that answer counts for every teacher. Your ban stays yours, though: your list only blocks songs in your room, and no answer from the checker ever unbans something you banned.
  • Your "Allow" stays yours too. If you let a song through, that only counts for your rooms. It never changes the archive for anyone else.

Students' privacy

  • No student accounts, no names, no emails. Nicknames are made up for them.
  • Students never touch Spotify, and no music streams to their Chromebooks. Only the teacher's computer plays it.
  • A room's songs, votes and nicknames are deleted when it closes (at the end of class, or the end of the day).
